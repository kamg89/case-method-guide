# Sources and instructional assumptions

The case date is **March 1, 2024**. The primary business source is [Warby Parker's 2023 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1504776/000150477624000006/wrby-20231231.htm), filed February 29, 2024. No subsequent outcomes are used to judge a recommendation.

| Reported measure | 2022 | 2023 | Filing location |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Net revenue, $m | 598.1 | 669.8 | Item 7, operating results; consolidated statements of operations |
| Gross profit, $m | 341.1 | 365.2 | Item 7, results of operations |
| Gross margin | 57.0% | 54.5% | Item 7, results of operations |
| Net loss, $m | (110.4) | (63.2) | Consolidated statements of operations |
| Stores at year end | 200 | 237 | Item 7, key business metrics/store count |
| Stores offering in-person eye exams | Not used | 194 | Item 1, business overview/retail stores |

Revenue growth: `(669.8 - 598.1) / 598.1 * 100 = 11.9879...%`, or **12.0%** to one decimal place. Store-count growth: `(237 - 200) / 200 * 100 = 18.5%`. These are company revenue and year-end footprint changes, not causal estimates or new-store returns. Parentheses denote losses.

The filing describes customers using physical and digital channels and management's view of stores as a source of awareness/acquisition. Customer journeys are illustrated without inventing a conversion rate or causal attribution. Management claims are labeled as such. The 194-store exam count is a reported fact, not an estimate of the benefit of exams.

The following are original instructional constructions: the advisory assignment, a choice about the next increment of investment, the three competing priorities, possible investment mechanisms, missing-evidence questions, all objections and simulated contributions, and the annotated recommendations. No fictional executive quote is attributed to a real person. Exhibit 3 is explicitly instructional analysis. The public evidence does not supply a budget, site forecast, matched-market experiment, or channel-specific return model; the app does not invent these as company facts.

The approximately 939-word narrative is original prose. It discusses strategy through customer value, incremental economics, capability fit, reversibility and opportunity cost. It is divided into short reading pages, followed by three exhibits. The original source is optional; all evidence needed to complete the exercise appears inside the app.

Broad case-method principles were informed by William Ellet, *The Case Study Handbook: A Student's Guide*, revised edition, Harvard Business Review Press, 2018, supplied privately by the instructor. That book, the supplied Harvard screenshots, Harvard cases, illustrations, video, and transcripts are not distributed here. The case, sequence, exercises, feedback and design were independently authored. This is not a lightly renamed version of the supplied restaurant case.
